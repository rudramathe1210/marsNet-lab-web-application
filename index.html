DTN (Delay-Tolerant Networking)
An architecture designed for networks subject to severe delays, frequent disruptions, and lack of continuous end-to-end connectivity. Standardized under CCSDS and IETF RFC 4838 / RFC 9171.
Areostationary Orbit
A circular synchronous orbit around Mars with an orbital period equal to one Martian Sol (24h 39m 35s), situated at an altitude of approximately 17,032 km above the Martian equator.
OWLT (One-Way Light Time)
The time required for an electromagnetic signal to propagate from transmitter to receiver across the vacuum of space at the speed of light (c ≈ 299,792 km/s). Between Earth and Mars, OWLT varies between 3.03 and 22.28 minutes.
Optical Depth (τ)
A measure of atmospheric opacity. On Mars, normal clear skies have τ ≈ 0.2–0.5. During severe global dust storms, τ exceeds 4.0 to 6.0, extinguishing over 99% of optical laser transmission.
CCSDS (Consultative Committee for Space Data Systems)
International forum formed in 1982 by major space agencies (NASA, ESA, ISRO, JAXA) that establishes interoperable communication standards for deep space exploration.
DSN (Deep Space Network)
NASA's international array of giant 34m and 70m parabolic antenna facilities located in Goldstone (California), Madrid (Spain), and Canberra (Australia), spaced ~120° apart to provide uninterrupted interplanetary tracking.

1. EXECUTIVE MISSION SUMMARY

The Internet we use today was engineered exclusively for Earth's environment: dense terrestrial fiber backbones, negligible sub-second round-trip latencies, GPS satellite synchronization, and benign atmosphere. As humanity establishes permanent scientific habitats and cities on Mars, these systems cannot simply be copy-pasted.

MarsNet Lab provides an AI-guided simulation, modeling, and educational testbed that prepares the next generation of planetary network engineers to conquer deep space delay, 0.38g orbital mechanics, and planet-scale dust storms.

SUPPORTING PLANETARY DATA
  • 0.38g Gravity: Areostationary altitude compressed to 17,032 km vs Earth's 35,786 km. Lower ΔV required for orbital correction.
  • Dust Storm Extremes: Planet-encircling storms elevate optical depth τ past 5.0, halting optical laser links and dropping solar generation by 80-90%.
  • Zero Legacy Infrastructure: No undersea cables, fiber routes, or cell towers exist. Complete ground-up architecture required.
  • Interplanetary Delay: 3.03 to 22.28 minutes one-way propagation creates 6 to 44 minute RTT, invalidating TCP handshakes.
POTENTIAL STAKEHOLDERS & USERS
  • NASA SCaN: Space Communications and Navigation architecture validation.
  • ESA (European Space Agency): ExoMars and Moonlight communication compatibility.
  • ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation): Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan) relay frameworks.
  • Space Education Institutes: Universities, aerospace engineering labs, and STEM students worldwide.
